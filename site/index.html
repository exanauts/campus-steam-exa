How a campus steam network carries a hard week

Scenario

Playback is precomputed solver output, not live plant data. Example University is fictional and its layout is generated. Only the steam supply side is drawn. Shed load is shown by building type, because the split between buildings of one type is not unique.

What you are looking at

The plant makes steam that travels through underground supply pipes to the buildings, gives up its heat there, and returns as water. Along the way friction lowers the pressure and the ground cools the steam, so what leaves the plant is not what arrives. For every hour of the week, the model solves for the flows, pressures and temperatures that satisfy this physics across the whole network at once. The optimizer minimizes the heat the plant must supply and the load left unserved, with the most important buildings penalized most when they are shed, while holding the plant to its rating and its hour-to-hour ramp limit. Each scenario is the same model solved again with the weather, the plant capacity or the pipe network changed.

How the data is handled

  • Read-only extract. Data is copied out of the historian. Nothing in the plant is changed.
  • One way out. The file moves in one direction, out of the plant network.
  • No inbound connections. Nothing connects into the plant network.
  • No commands to equipment. This work never sends a setpoint or command to any device.
  • Results as reports. Findings reach operators as reports, and operators decide what to do with them.

CPU versus GPU

Where the GPU may pay off later

None of the following has been run, and no speedup is claimed for any of them.

  • Not run A full-year horizon solved as one problem.
  • Not run Many outage cases solved as one batch.
  • Not run Networks much larger than this campus.
  • Not run GPU linear solver settings tuned to reach full tolerance on long horizons.

Limits

This is a planning model on invented data. It does not describe any real plant.

  • Steady state each hour. Hours are linked only by the plant ramp limit. There are no pipe transients.
  • No valves. Flow splits follow from the physics and the optimization alone.
  • Synthetic data. The campus, weather, demand and equipment are all generated.
  • Sensible cooling only. Steam loses heat without condensing, so the model has no condensation in pipes.
  • Saturation fixed at 100 C, as in the reference paper, regardless of pressure.
  • Some quantities are not unique. Water-side pressures, return-side temperatures and flows in lightly loaded pipes can differ between equally good solutions, so they are not predictions. The animation fades lightly loaded pipes for this reason and does not draw the water side.
