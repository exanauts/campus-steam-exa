# Site data

Files generated by `scripts/run_site.jl` for the public site. All data is synthetic and describes the
fictional Example University.

## Scenarios

`scripts/site/scenarios.jl` writes these files to `site/data/scenarios/`:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `network.json` | Junctions, pipes, loads and plant |
| `baseline.json` | A normal winter week |
| `cold_snap.json` | The same week with every load 50 percent higher for 48 hours |
| `plant_derate.json` | The generated cold-snap week with one of four boilers out for 48 hours |
| `pipe_out.json` | The normal week with steam main pipe 20 out of service for 48 hours |

Each hour is a steady state, and the hours are coupled only by the plant ramp limit. The model has no
pipe transients and no valves. Every file carries `synthetic: true`, `data_label` and `model_label`;
show those labels with the results.

### Scenario definitions

| Scenario | Hours of the synthetic year | Event, in hours of the scenario (1 to 168) |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 1177 to 1344 | None |
| `cold_snap` | 1177 to 1344 | Loads at 150 percent in hours 49 to 96, building over hours 46 to 48 and fading over hours 97 to 99 |
| `plant_derate` | 889 to 1056 | Rating 82.5 MW instead of 110 MW in hours 25 to 72 |
| `pipe_out` | 1177 to 1344 | Pipe 20 out of service in hours 49 to 96 |

The cold snap builds over three hours because a 50 percent step is 30.3 MW, more than the 27.5 MW/h
ramp limit. With a step, the model shed 0.98 MWh at the end of the surge because of the ramp limit alone.

### `network.json`

Each group is a set of parallel arrays.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `junctions` | `id`, `side`, `kind`, `x_m`, `y_m` |
| `pipes` | `id`, `side`, `from`, `to`, `length_m`, `diameter_m`, `is_loop` |
| `loads` | `id`, `name`, `type`, `steam_junction`, `design_heat_MW`, `priority_weight` |
| `plant` | `steam_junction`, `rating_MW`, `n_boilers`, `one_boiler_out_MW`, `ramp_limit_MW_per_h` |

### `<scenario>.json`

Hour arrays have one entry per hour. Matrices are indexed `[hour][element]`, with elements in the order
of the neighboring `ids`.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `schema_version`, `scenario`, `title`, `description` | Identification |
| `hours`, `first_hour_of_synthetic_year` | Length and position of the week |
| `events` | Scenario events, with `first_hour` and `last_hour` counted from 1 |
| `solver` | `status`, `converged_at_tolerance`, `tolerance`, `iterations`, `solve_time_s`, `nlp_solves`, `orientation_method` |
| `presented` | `false` when the solve did not reach `SOLVE_SUCCEEDED` at the tolerance |
| `summary` | `peak_output_MW`, `total_demand_MWh`, `total_shed_MWh`, `critical_shed_MWh`, `total_losses_MWh`, `total_excess_MWh`, `buildings_shed` |
| `plant` | Hour arrays `output_MW`, `rating_MW`, `demand_MW`, `shed_MW`, `excess_MW`, `losses_MW` |
| `nodes` | Steam junctions only: `ids`, `pressure_kPa`, `temperature_C`, `not_unique_hours` |
| `pipes` | `ids`, `flow_kg_s`, `loss_kW`, `flow_not_unique_hours` |
| `loads` | `ids`, `shed_MW`, `excess_MW` |
| `uniqueness` | How far each quantity can be trusted, as described below |
| `flow_not_unique_below_kg_s` | The flow limit used for the hour marks |

When `presented` is `false`, the file has only the identification, `events` and `solver` fields. Show
the status and no numbers.

Flows are signed: a negative flow runs against the `from -> to` direction in `network.json`. Pipe values
are `null` in hours when the pipe is out of service. `buildings_shed` lists buildings with at least
0.01 MWh of shed load, each with `load`, `name`, `type`, `shed_MWh` and `peak_shed_MW`. In
`plant_derate`, `peak_output_MW` occurs outside the boiler outage.

`plant_derate.json` adds `priority_comparison`:

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `note` | How to read the comparison |
| `cases` | The derate week with the priority weights by building type, and with a uniform weight of 10 |
| `deeper_derate` | A stress case with one boiler running (27.5 MW) for 9 peak hours, with the same two `cases` |

Each case has `label`, `weights`, `status`, `presented`, `iterations`, `solve_time_s`, `summary` (without
`buildings_shed`) and `shed_by_type_MWh`.

`pipe_out.json` adds `reroute`, which compares the outage hours with the same hours of `baseline`:
`pipe_out`, `first_hour`, `last_hour`, `reference_scenario`, `reversed_steam_pipes` (`pipe`, `hours`) and
`largest_flow_changes` (`pipe`, `reference_mean_kg_s`, `outage_mean_kg_s`). A pipe counts as reversed in
an hour when its flow changes sign and is at least 0.5 kg/s in size in both cases.

### Quantities that are not unique

Many operating points are almost equally good, so some values are not predictions (see
`docs/validation-notes.md`). The `uniqueness` block gives one label per quantity:

| Quantity | Label | Use on the site |
|---|---|---|
| `plant_output`, `total_shed`, `shed_by_type` | `unique` | Headline results |
| `total_losses` | `indicative` | Headline, rounded to whole MWh |
| `steam_temperature`, `steam_pipe_flow`, `steam_pipe_loss` | `unique_except_marked_hours` | Draw, and fade the marked hours |
| `steam_pressure` | `indicative_except_marked_hours` | Draw as a pattern, with no precise readings |
| `shed_by_building` | `not_unique_within_equal_weights` | Name the building type; treat individual buildings as examples |
| `water_pipe_flow`, `water_pipe_loss` | `not_unique` | Do not draw conclusions from them |
| `water_pressure`, `water_temperature` | `not_exported` | Not in the files |

The hour marks are lists of `[first, last]` hour ranges keyed by id as a string:

- `pipes.flow_not_unique_hours`: steam pipes carrying less than 0.5 kg/s in that hour.
- `nodes.not_unique_hours`: steam junctions where every connected steam pipe is below that flow.

The marks are deliberately cautious. Some marked pipes are building laterals whose flow follows the
building's demand. They cover 29 to 36 percent of steam pipe-hours and 31 to 39 percent of steam
node-hours.

To measure this, every scenario was solved a second time at tolerance 1e-10 with no bound relaxation and
a different scaling limit. These are the largest differences between the two solves across the four
scenarios, for hours that are not marked:

| Quantity | Largest difference |
|---|---|
| Plant output | 2.3 kW |
| Steam temperature | 0.40 K |
| Steam pressure | 1.7 kPa |
| Steam pipe flow | 0.061 kg/s |
| Steam pipe loss | 57 W |
| Total losses over the week | 0.54 percent |
| Total shed, derate week | below 0.0001 MWh |
| Water pipe flow | 9.9 kg/s, which is why it is labeled `not_unique` |

### Reading the priority comparison

With one boiler out, no critical load is shed under either weighting, so that case does not show the
weights protecting critical load. There the weights decide whether labs are shed. The `deeper_derate`
stress case is the one that shows critical load being protected. Show it as a stress case, separate from
the four scenarios.
