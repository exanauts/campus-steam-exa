# Site data

Files generated by `scripts/run_site.jl` for the public site. All data is synthetic and describes the
fictional Example University.

## Scenarios

`scripts/site/scenarios.jl` writes these files to `site/data/scenarios/`:

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `network.json` | Junctions, pipes, loads and plant |
| `baseline.json` | A normal winter week |
| `cold_snap.json` | The same week with every load 50 percent higher for 48 hours |
| `plant_derate.json` | The week the weather generator flags as its cold snap, with one of four boilers out for 48 hours. This is not the week of `cold_snap.json` |
| `pipe_out.json` | The normal week with steam main pipe 4 out of service for 48 hours |

Each hour is a steady state, and the hours are coupled only by the plant ramp limit. The model has no
pipe transients and no valves. Every file carries `synthetic: true`, `data_label` and `model_label`;
show those labels with the results.

### Scenario definitions

| Scenario | Hours of the synthetic year | Event, in hours of the scenario (1 to 168) |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 1177 to 1344 | None |
| `cold_snap` | 1177 to 1344 | Loads at 150 percent in hours 49 to 96, building over hours 46 to 48 and fading over hours 97 to 99 |
| `plant_derate` | 889 to 1056 | Rating 82.5 MW instead of 110 MW in hours 25 to 72 |
| `pipe_out` | 1177 to 1344 | Pipe 4 out of service in hours 49 to 96 |

`cold_snap` and `plant_derate` use different weeks. `cold_snap` multiplies the demand of the normal winter
week by a load surge. `plant_derate` keeps the unscaled demand of the week that the weather generator flags
as its cold snap (`is_cold_snap`), whose weather is colder on average than the normal week.

The cold snap builds over three hours because a 50 percent step is 30.3 MW, more than the 27.5 MW/h
ramp limit. With a step, the model shed 0.98 MWh at the end of the surge because of the ramp limit alone.

### `network.json`

Each group is a set of parallel arrays.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `junctions` | `id`, `side`, `kind`, `x_m`, `y_m` |
| `pipes` | `id`, `side`, `from`, `to`, `length_m`, `diameter_m`, `is_loop` |
| `loads` | `id`, `name`, `type`, `steam_junction`, `design_heat_MW`, `priority_weight` |
| `plant` | `steam_junction`, `rating_MW`, `n_boilers`, `one_boiler_out_MW`, `ramp_limit_MW_per_h` |

### `<scenario>.json`

Hour arrays have one entry per hour. Matrices are indexed `[hour][element]`, with elements in the order
of the neighboring `ids`.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `schema_version`, `scenario`, `title`, `description` | Identification |
| `hours`, `first_hour_of_synthetic_year` | Length and position of the week |
| `events` | Scenario events, with `first_hour` and `last_hour` counted from 1 |
| `solver` | `status`, `converged_at_tolerance`, `tolerance`, `iterations`, `solve_time_s`, `nlp_solves`, `orientation_method` |
| `presented` | `false` when the solve did not reach `SOLVE_SUCCEEDED` at the tolerance |
| `summary` | `peak_output_MW`, `total_demand_MWh`, `total_shed_MWh`, `critical_shed_MWh`, `total_losses_MWh`, `total_excess_MWh`, `buildings_shed` |
| `plant` | Hour arrays `output_MW`, `rating_MW`, `demand_MW`, `shed_MW`, `excess_MW`, `losses_MW` |
| `nodes` | Steam junctions only: `ids`, `pressure_kPa`, `temperature_C`, `not_unique_hours` |
| `pipes` | `ids`, `flow_kg_s`, `loss_kW`, `flow_not_unique_hours` |
| `loads` | `ids`, `shed_MW`, `excess_MW` |
| `uniqueness` | How far each quantity can be trusted, as described below |
| `flow_not_unique_below_kg_s` | The flow limit used for the hour marks |

When `presented` is `false`, the file has only the identification, `events` and `solver` fields. Show
the status and no numbers.

The timeline under the map draws `plant.demand_MW`, `plant.output_MW` and `plant.rating_MW`, shades the
hours where `plant.shed_MW` is at least 0.01 MW, and marks each entry of `events`. The headline is built
from `summary.total_shed_MWh`, `summary.critical_shed_MWh`, the shed by building type summed from
`loads.shed_MW`, the event fields (`kind`, `factor`, `pipe`) and, for `pipe_out`, `reroute`.

Flows are signed: a negative flow runs against the `from -> to` direction in `network.json`. The map draws
an arrow at the middle of each loaded steam pipe from this sign, and none on faded or out-of-service pipes.
Pipe values are `null` in hours when the pipe is out of service. `buildings_shed` lists buildings with at least
0.01 MWh of shed load, each with `load`, `name`, `type`, `shed_MWh` and `peak_shed_MW`. In
`plant_derate`, `peak_output_MW` occurs outside the boiler outage.

`plant_derate.json` adds `priority_comparison`:

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `note` | How to read the comparison |
| `cases` | The derate week with the priority weights by building type, and with a uniform weight of 10 |
| `deeper_derate` | A stress case with one boiler running (27.5 MW) for 9 peak hours, with the same two `cases` |

Each case has `label`, `weights`, `status`, `presented`, `iterations`, `solve_time_s`, `summary` (without
`buildings_shed`) and `shed_by_type_MWh`.

`pipe_out.json` adds `reroute`, which compares the outage hours with the same hours of `baseline`:
`pipe_out`, `first_hour`, `last_hour`, `reference_scenario`, `reversed_steam_pipes` (`pipe`, `hours`) and
`largest_flow_changes` (`pipe`, `reference_mean_kg_s`, `outage_mean_kg_s`). A pipe counts as reversed in
an hour when its flow changes sign and is at least 0.5 kg/s in size in both cases.

### Quantities that are not unique

Many operating points are almost equally good, so some values are not predictions (see
`docs/validation-notes.md`). The `uniqueness` block gives one label per quantity:

| Quantity | Label | Use on the site |
|---|---|---|
| `plant_output`, `total_shed`, `shed_by_type` | `unique` | Headline results |
| `total_losses` | `indicative` | Headline, rounded to whole MWh |
| `steam_temperature`, `steam_pipe_flow`, `steam_pipe_loss` | `unique_except_marked_hours` | Draw, and fade the marked hours |
| `steam_pressure` | `indicative_except_marked_hours` | Draw as a pattern, with no precise readings |
| `shed_by_building` | `not_unique_within_equal_weights` | Name the building type; treat individual buildings as examples |
| `water_pipe_flow`, `water_pipe_loss` | `not_unique` | Do not draw conclusions from them |
| `water_pressure`, `water_temperature` | `not_exported` | Not in the files |

The hour marks are lists of `[first, last]` hour ranges keyed by id as a string:

- `pipes.flow_not_unique_hours`: steam pipes carrying less than 0.5 kg/s in that hour.
- `nodes.not_unique_hours`: steam junctions where every connected steam pipe is below that flow.

The marks are deliberately cautious. Some marked pipes are building laterals whose flow follows the
building's demand. They cover 29 to 36 percent of steam pipe-hours and 31 to 39 percent of steam
node-hours.

To measure this, every scenario was solved a second time at tolerance 1e-10 with no bound relaxation and
a different scaling limit. These are the largest differences between the two solves across the four
scenarios, for hours that are not marked:

| Quantity | Largest difference |
|---|---|
| Plant output | 2.3 kW |
| Steam temperature | 0.40 K |
| Steam pressure | 1.7 kPa |
| Steam pipe flow | 0.061 kg/s |
| Steam pipe loss | 57 W |
| Total losses over the week | 0.54 percent |
| Total shed, derate week | below 0.0001 MWh |
| Water pipe flow | 9.9 kg/s, which is why it is labeled `not_unique` |

### Reading the priority comparison

With one boiler out, no critical load is shed under either weighting, so that case does not show the
weights protecting critical load. There the weights decide whether labs are shed. The `deeper_derate`
stress case is the one that shows critical load being protected. Show it as a stress case, separate from
the four scenarios.

## Calibration

`scripts/site/calibration.jl` defines `run_calibration(outdir)`, which writes `calibration.json` and
`tag_template.csv` to `site/data/`. All data is synthetic: the historian-style CSV is generated at build
time from a simulated network whose true coefficients are known, and is deleted after the run.

### Method

- **Network.** The synthetic 40-junction campus from `scaled_campus(40)`.
- **True model.** Heat-loss and friction multipliers per group are set by `TRUE_GROUP_LOSS` and
  `TRUE_GROUP_FRICTION` in `src/calibration.jl`, times a seeded pipe-to-pipe spread (`TRUE_PIPE_SPREAD`).
  The score compares each estimate with the length-weighted mean of the true multipliers in its group.
- **Historian file.** Hourly narrow CSV with the columns `DateTime,TagName,Value,Quality`. Rows have
  sensor noise. Independently per tag and hour, 1% of rows are absent, 2% are flagged `Bad` and 0.5% are
  flagged `Uncertain`. About six plant-wide gaps per year of 4 to 12 hours have no rows.
- **Ingest.** `read_historian` and `align_historian` in `src/historian.jl`. Only `Good` samples are used.
  Excluded and missing samples are not filled in and add no term to the objective.
- **Inverse problem.** One ExaModels NLP over all hours, with the hourly network states, the hourly heat
  of each building, and one heat-loss and one friction multiplier per group shared across hours.
  Condensate-return multipliers are held at nominal, because the tags do not determine them.
- **Grid.** Tag sets are nested: the set of size `n` is the first `n` curated tags. One month is 730 hours.
  The site build runs the points in `CAL_CI_POINTS` within `CAL_BUDGET_S`.

### `calibration.json`

| Key | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `synthetic` | Bool | Always `true` |
| `data_note` | String | Statement that the data is synthetic |
| `network` | Object | `junctions`, `pipes`, `loads`, `description` |
| `network_used` | Object | `name`, `generator_call`, `is_demo_campus` (always `false`), `note` |
| `findings` | Array of String | Statements the site shows with these results. The statement about months of data is built from the converged 25-tag cells of this run |
| `seasonal_check` | Object | The 25-tag, one-month fit in month 1 and month 7, see below |
| `historian` | Object | `format` (column names), `hours`, `tags`, `rows`, `file_bytes`, `tag_hours`, `good`, `excluded_bad`, `excluded_uncertain`, `excluded_blank`, `absent`, `good_percent` |
| `true_coefficients` | Object | `groups`, `estimated` (Bool per group), `loss_multiplier`, `friction_multiplier` (per group), `pipe_spread_percent` |
| `tags_by_category` | Object | For each category, a list of `name`, `unit`, `description` |
| `tag_sets` | Object | For each tag count, the tag names in that set |
| `bad_quality_example` | Object | See below |
| `finding` | String or null | One sentence built from the converged grid points of this run |
| `chart` | Object or null | `title`, `subtitle`, `x_label`, `y_label`, `x_scale`, `y_scale`, and `series` (each with `name`, `x` as tag counts, `y` as `max_error_percent`). Only converged points appear |
| `ask` | Object | Size of the export: `tag_count`, `years`, `rows`, `size_label` |
| `sample_rows` | Array | The rows of `bad_quality_example` as `DateTime`, `TagName`, `Value`, `Quality` |
| `identifiability` | Array | One entry per tag count, see below |
| `grid` | Object | `tag_counts`, `months`, `cells`, see below |
| `gpu` | Object | Outcome of the GPU attempt, see below |
| `timing_s` | Object | `generate_historian`, `read_historian`, `nominal_simulation`, `identifiability`, `total`, `budget` |

The page reads `network.junctions`, `true_coefficients.groups`, `estimated` and `pipe_spread_percent` for
the limits of the test, `seasonal_check` and the 25-tag `grid.cells` for the reason given next to the
length of the export, and `findings` for the expandable details. Values and units stay in SI in the file
(`K`, `Pa`); the page shows temperatures in C and pressures in kPa in the sample rows and the tag list.

**`bad_quality_example`** has `tag`, `unit`, `samples` (each with `time`, `value`, `quality`, `used`),
`mean_with_bad_sample`, `mean_after_exclusion` and `samples_excluded`.

**`identifiability`** entries are linearized predictions at the true coefficients, with unmetered hourly
quantities marginalized out. They assume that errors are independent from hour to hour, so they are a
lower bound on the real error and are not measured recovery errors. Each has `tags`,
`predicted_std_1_month` and `predicted_std_12_months` (each with `loss` and `friction`, by group, as a
fraction of the multiplier), `pipe_directions_resolved_12_months` (directions in the space of per-pipe
coefficients with a predicted standard deviation below 10%), `pipe_coefficients` and `sample_hours`.

**`grid.cells`** has one entry for each combination of tag count and months.

| Key | Present | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `tags`, `months`, `hours` | Always | Grid point |
| `run` | Always | `false` when the point was not run in this build |
| `reason` | Not run or not converged | Why there is no recovery error |
| `solver_status`, `converged`, `iterations` | Run | MadNLP outcome |
| `variables`, `constraints`, `jacobian_nonzeros`, `measurements` | Run | Problem size |
| `build_s`, `solve_s` | Run | Wall-clock seconds |
| `estimate` | Converged | `loss` and `friction` multipliers per group |
| `error_percent` | Converged | `loss` and `friction` relative error per estimated group |
| `max_error_percent` | Converged | Largest relative error over the estimated groups |

**`gpu`** has `attempted`, and `reason` when no attempt was made. After an attempt it has `tags`, `months`,
`wall_limit_s`, `device` and either the keys of a grid cell or `run = false`, `converged = false`, `reason`
and `error_message`.

**`seasonal_check`** has `run`, `tags`, `months`, `groups`, `true_loss_multiplier` and
`true_friction_multiplier`. When `run` is `true`, `windows` lists `label`, `first_hour`, `solver_status`,
`converged`, `solve_s` and, if converged, `estimate`, `error_percent`, `max_error_percent`, `max_error_group`
and `max_error_coefficient`. The errors compare each estimated group with its true group mean, as in the grid
cells. When `run` is `false`, `reason` says why.

### Why recovery error does not keep falling with more data

These are local diagnostic runs, not part of the site build, and the site shows none of these numbers.
Each uses 25 tags and a 12-month synthetic file, and each cell is the largest relative error in percent
over the four estimated groups. All 26 solves ended with `SOLVE_SUCCEEDED`. The site build generates a
24-month file, and the communication gaps depend on the file length, so its results differ slightly from
this baseline: the same fit at 3 months gives 1.149 there and 1.065 here.

| Run | What differs from the baseline | 1 month | 3 months | 6 months | 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Nothing | 1.668 | 1.065 | 2.144 | 3.052 |
| Condensate at truth | Condensate multipliers fixed at their true values | 1.668 | 1.065 | 2.144 | 3.052 |
| Tolerance 1e-8 | Solver tolerance tightened from 1e-6 | not run | 1.063 | 2.142 | not run |
| True heat prior | Building heat prior centered on the true heat | 1.590 | 1.246 | 2.361 | 3.214 |
| No sensor noise | Tag values are exact | 1.961 | 1.583 | 2.047 | 2.699 |
| No pipe spread | Pipes in a group share one true multiplier, condensate at truth | 2.256 | 0.998 | 1.256 | 1.030 |
| No spread, no noise | Both removed | 0.003 | 0.006 | 0.006 | 0.016 |

- **Cause.** The rise comes from the pipe-to-pipe spread inside each group. It persists without sensor
  noise, and it disappears when the spread is removed.
- **Ruled out.** Holding the condensate multipliers at nominal (estimates agree to all five reported
  decimals), solver tolerance, and the building heat prior.
- **Sensor noise** adds a separate error of about 1% that does not shrink between 3 and 12 months. Its
  mechanism has not been isolated.
- **Season.** Separate 3-month fits starting at months 0, 3, 6 and 9 give steam-mains friction multipliers
  of 1.2417, 1.2075, 1.1874 and 1.2100 against a true group mean of 1.2551. That the group value acts as
  an average whose weighting follows the flow pattern is an inference from these runs, not a direct
  measurement.
- **No estimator change was made.** A lumped condensate term would not help, because the condensate
  multipliers do not affect these results. Finer coefficient groups were not tried.

### `tag_template.csv`

A fill-in tag list with the columns `tag_name`, `category`, `unit`, `description`. The rows are the
generic names of the curated synthetic tags, to be replaced with a site's own tag names.

## Benchmarks

`scripts/site/benchmarks.jl` defines `run_benchmarks(outdir)`, which writes `benchmarks.json` and
`benchmarks.csv` to `site/data/`. All data is synthetic. The deployed files hold only the solves of the CI sweep
of that build. The local sweep is committed in `results/`, is explained in [`docs/gpu-notes.md`](gpu-notes.md),
and is not shown on the site.

### Sweeps

| Sweep | Network sizes, one period | Periods on the demo campus | Repeats | Strict tolerances | Ipopt | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CI | Demo campus, 200 and 400 junctions | T = 24 | 3 | 1e-8 | No | `site/data/benchmarks.csv`, rebuilt by every site build |
| Local | Demo campus, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600 and 800 junctions | T = 24, 168 and 720 | 5 | 1e-6 and 1e-8 | Yes | `results/benchmarks.csv`, committed with its commit and date |

The CI sweep stops starting new solves once 270 s have passed and lists what it skipped in `not_run`. Run the
local sweep with `julia --project=scripts scripts/site/benchmarks.jl local`. The calibration problem is not
solved here. Its outcome is read from `calibration.json`, so the calibration step must run first.

### Solvers and modes

| `solver` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `cpu_madnlp_mumps` | MadNLP with MUMPS, one thread |
| `gpu_madnlp_cudss` | MadNLP with cuDSS on the GPU, synchronous so that linear solver time is reported |
| `gpu_madnlp_cudss_async` | The same with asynchronous cuDSS, the package default. Linear solver time is not available |
| `cpu_ipopt_mumps` | Ipopt with MUMPS on the same model, local sweep only |

| `mode` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `default` | Solver defaults at a tolerance of 1e-8, so a solve may stop at the acceptable level |
| `strict` | Acceptable termination off and a bound relaxation of 1e-8 on every device, so success means the requested tolerance was met |

### `benchmarks.csv`

One row per solve. `results/benchmarks.csv` has the same columns.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `run_profile`, `commit`, `run_date` | Sweep, source commit and UTC start time |
| `case`, `family`, `junctions`, `periods` | Benchmark case. `family` is `network_size` or `horizon` |
| `solver`, `mode`, `tol_requested`, `repeat` | See above |
| `nvar`, `ncon`, `nnzj`, `nnzh` | Variables, constraints, and Jacobian and Hessian nonzeros |
| `status`, `iterations` | Solver outcome |
| `build_s`, `total_s`, `linear_s`, `eval_s`, `other_s` | Model build, solve, linear solver, function evaluation and remaining time, in seconds |
| `compile_s` | Compile time recorded inside the timed section |
| `host_load_1min` | One-minute host load average when the run started |
| `objective` | Objective at the returned point, in the model's scaled units |
| `primal_residual`, `dual_residual`, `complementarity_residual`, `kkt_residual` | Residuals recomputed on the unscaled model by one function for every solver |
| `objective_rel`, `plant_output_rel`, `steam_pressure_rel`, `steam_temperature_rel`, `steam_flow_rel` | Agreement with the CPU reference solution, as the largest relative difference |
| `nonunique_water_pressure_rel`, `nonunique_return_temperature_rel`, `nonunique_light_flow_rel` | The same for quantities that are not unique at the optimum, so these do not measure accuracy. See `docs/validation-notes.md` |
| `gpu_other_processes` | Other compute processes seen on the GPU during the run |
| `error` | First line of the error when a run failed |

`steam_flow_rel` covers steam pipes that carry at least 1% of the plant flow. Lighter pipes on either side are
counted in `nonunique_light_flow_rel`.

### `benchmarks.json`

| Key | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `label` | String | Statement that the data is synthetic |
| `profile`, `sweep`, `sweep_runtime_s` | String, Object, Number | The sweep that produced this file and its runtime |
| `environment` | Object | Commit, date, device, driver, Julia and package versions, CPU model and thread counts |
| `solver_settings`, `agreement_note` | Object, String | Settings of each solver and how agreement is measured |
| `size_label`, `runs`, `notes` | String, Array, String | Points drawn by the site, see below |
| `summary` | Array | Median, minimum and maximum per case, solver, mode and tolerance |
| `like_for_like` | Array | CPU and GPU at the same strict tolerance, see below |
| `crossover` | Object | `network_size`, `horizon` and `tolerance`. A case name, or `null` when no crossover was observed |
| `calibration` | Object or null | CPU and GPU outcomes at 25 tags, read from `calibration.json` |
| `time_to_first_solve`, `gpu_memory` | Array | Fresh-process measurements. Local sweep only |
| `not_run` | Array | Each entry has `item` or a case, and `reason` |
| `records` | Array | The rows of `benchmarks.csv` |

**`runs`** come from the sweep that wrote the file, at the tightest strict tolerance. Each has `size`
(variables), `case`, `family`, `device`, `solve_s`, `solve_s_min`, `solve_s_max`, `iterations`, `solved_repeats`,
`repeats`, `planned_repeats`, `status`, `residual_ratio` and `residual_limit`. `repeats` counts the repeats that
ran on that device; `planned_repeats` minus `repeats` were not run, for example when the build's time budget ran out.
Times and iterations are medians over the repeats that solved, and are `null` when no repeat solved. The status is
`solved` only if every repeat that ran met the tolerance and, on the GPU, the KKT residual was at most
`residual_limit` (twice) the CPU's. Otherwise it is `partial`, and the site states the reason for that point.
`residual_ratio` is the GPU's largest KKT residual over the CPU's, over solved repeats only, and is `null` for the
CPU and when the two cannot be compared.

**`like_for_like`** entries have `case`, `family`, `junctions`, `periods`, `nvar`, `tol_requested`, `repeats`,
`cpu_statuses`, `gpu_statuses`, `cpu_solved_repeats`, `gpu_solved_repeats`, `both_solved_every_repeat`,
`cpu_total_s`, `gpu_total_s`, `cpu_iterations`, `gpu_iterations`, `cpu_kkt_residual`, `gpu_kkt_residual`,
`gpu_max_objective_rel`, `gpu_over_cpu_time`, `gpu_slowest_over_cpu_median`, `gpu_over_cpu_kkt_residual` and
`achieved_tolerance_matched`. Times and iterations cover the repeats that solved.
